# Miah — Vision

## Why we're doing this

Planned work often needs more than one agent to get done — implementation, review, verification, each with different strengths. Let loose on their own, agents drift, stall, and fail quietly, and "it's done" is just words. The result: you can't tell whether the work is actually going in the right direction until it's too late.

Miah exists so you never have to gamble on that.

## Who it's for

People who direct AI agents to do real work and need to be able to trust the outcome — engineers, technical leads, and operators who approve plans and are accountable for the results.

## What Miah is

A way to orchestrate the implementation of planned work across multiple specialist agents. You approve a plan; Miah directs it, step by step, from start to finish. It never builds the work itself — it coordinates the builders and the reviewers, and nothing counts as done until the work is genuinely right and you've signed off on it.

## What it looks like

A plan goes in. Trusted, accounted-for work comes out. Every step is delegated, reviewed, and approved in order. You can watch it as it goes, step in when you want, and stop it the moment it heads the wrong way.

## What success looks like

- Work is **observable** — you can always see what's happening, where things stand, and what's been spent.
- Work is **auditable** — every step leaves a trace: what was done, by whom, and why.
- Work is **evidence-based, with receipts** — results come with proof, not promises.
- Work is **resumable** — a crashed session doesn't lose a step; pick up exactly where things left off.
- **Failures are accounted for** — nothing fails silently. Problems are visible, recorded, and traced.
- You can **determine direction and stop early** — if execution is headed the wrong way, you can halt it before it costs more.

## What it's not

- Not a tool for exploring ideas or writing plans — it executes plans that are already approved.
- Not an agent that builds things itself — it directs the specialists.
- Not a black box — nothing happens invisibly or without a trace.

## Where it goes next

Start with one plan, executed step by step, verified and approved. Over time, grow into parallel workstreams, multiple concurrent runs, and richer accounting — without ever losing the core promise: trusted, traceable work.
